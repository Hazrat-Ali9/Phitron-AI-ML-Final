# Premises Rent Control Act 1991 — Plain-Language Summary

A short summary of the main points of the Premises Rent Control Act, 1991 (বাড়ি ভাড়া নিয়ন্ত্রণ আইন, ১৯৯১) that matter to tenants and landlords in Dhaka.

**This is general information, not legal advice.** For a real dispute, talk to a lawyer or the House Rent Controller's office.

## Rent receipts (রসিদ)

The landlord must give the tenant a written receipt, signed by the landlord, for every rent payment. The tenant should keep these receipts. A digital record with a bKash/Nagad transaction ID, such as the BariBhara rent ledger, helps both sides prove what was paid.

## Advance (অগ্রিম) and premium

The Act says a landlord should not take more than one month's rent as an advance, and must not demand any extra "premium" or "salami" for giving the flat, unless the tenant agrees to more. In practice many agreements in Dhaka ask for two or more months of advance by mutual agreement; whatever is agreed should be written in the lease and refunded when the tenant leaves.

## Standard rent and increases

The Act refers to a "standard rent" that is fair for the property. Rent should not be increased more often than once every two years. If a tenant believes the rent is unreasonable, they can apply to the House Rent Controller to fix the standard rent.

## Paying rent on time

Rent is normally paid by the 15th of the following month under the Act, unless the agreement says otherwise (BariBhara agreements use the 7th). If the landlord refuses to accept rent, the tenant can send it by money order or deposit it with the Rent Controller, so they are not treated as being in default.

## Eviction (উচ্ছেদ)

A landlord cannot evict a tenant who pays rent regularly and follows the agreement, except for reasons allowed by law, such as: rent not paid on time, subletting without permission, using the flat for illegal or nuisance purposes, damaging the property, or the landlord genuinely needing the premises for their own use. The landlord must follow the legal process and give proper notice. Cutting off water, electricity or gas, or locking the tenant out to force them to leave, is not allowed.

## Repairs

The landlord must keep the building in a fit and habitable condition. If the landlord does not do a necessary repair after being asked, the tenant may in some cases get the repair done and adjust the cost with the Rent Controller's permission.

## Where to complain

Disputes about rent, receipts, advance or eviction go to the House Rent Controller (in Dhaka, under the office of the Deputy Commissioner). Many problems can be solved first by talking to the owner or the building committee.
